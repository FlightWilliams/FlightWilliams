# Boss Entrances

By Will Wang. Contains every encounter entrance.

## Fogtown

### 1 · Night Watch Noah · Start with the Last Stretch

Night Watch Noah first explains the town’s predicament. Since the Ash Works caught fire, the town has been trapped in this one night. Some roads bring you back where you started, and memories lose pieces. He does not know how it began; he only knows that paper and recordings are more reliable than memory.

He sets tea beside the file and returns the portable lantern from the desk. Its rim is dented, with soot in one corner of the glass. You cannot remember the roads it traveled, but your hand settles firmly around its worn handle. With the power out, you need light to read the map and watch your footing.

“I saw you bring it back to the duty room. I did not see you disappear,” Noah says. “For anything earlier, we need witnesses. Abel knows the bridge roads. Finn carried messages for the post office.” Recognizing the lantern does not let him invent the rest of your night.

You ask about the people wrapped in blankets in the photograph. Noah marks the bridge, post office and duty room while you write your questions by lamplight. You take both lantern and file: establish your return, then find the people who once followed its light.

### 2 · Broken Horn Abel · Place the Photograph on the Map

Abel measures the railing in the photograph and turns the map toward the window. “This road leads to the evacuation transfer point, not the cemetery.”

You will check the traffic recorded along it. The photograph offers a location, not yet an answer about who was hurt or who returned.

### 3 · Night Watch Noah · What the Water Was For

A water delivery slip lies among the patrol notes. Noah points to its purpose: drinking water for evacuees, not cleaning personal effects.

“Keep this small fact. Someone was caring for living people.” You mark the delivery point beside the transfer area and continue checking the accounts.

### 4 · Broken Horn Abel · The Direction Back to Town

At the bridge Abel asks a rooftop repairer which way the traveler went. The answer is limited: someone in your coat walked toward town and stopped to rest.

“Returning is not disappearing.” Abel draws an inward arrow without inventing where that traveler had been before.

### 5 · Empty Hands Finn · Separate the Three Papers

Finn retrieves the transit slip and drops a stray advertisement back into the bin. “The file, envelope and transit slip each tell us something different.”

He knows the post office stamps and can trace the material. As for its sender, he tells you to ignore the ready-made answers being sold outside.

### 6 · Night Watch Noah · The Order in the Duty Book

Noah sends the patrol page, signing only what he witnessed: a traveler reached the street and later came to the duty room door.

You place it after the bridge sighting. The accounts connect, but do not explain the exhaustion. That question lies farther back along the evacuation route.

### 7 · Empty Hands Finn · A Death Notice for Sale

Someone by the newsstand calls the missing notice an obituary and offers to sell you a “safe route.” Finn points out that it has neither a deceased-person field nor a certifying witness.

“He is selling your fear.” You keep the original and refuse to change course on an unsupported claim.

### 8 · Empty Hands Finn · Finn Keeps the Whole Envelope

Finn waits behind the post office with your envelope. You set the lantern on a dry crate. He removes the blue thread from the lining, then unfolds the lining in front of you, leaving no hidden pocket.

“People used to ask me to deliver only the page they wanted seen. This time, look at all of it.” The light reveals the backward knot, like the one at your cuff. Your fingers recognize an old sewing habit.

Does that identify the sender? Finn shakes his head: coats can be borrowed and thread can change hands. Seeing clearly is not the same as establishing a fact. He has you copy the intact envelope’s transit stamps beside the map before packing the original. The lantern lights the road; dates and independent sightings must connect it.

### 9 · Night Watch Noah · The Stretcher Stopped at the Street

A transport note records an exhausted traveler taken to the street outside the duty room, insisting on walking the last steps. Noah recognizes the handover point.

“That matches where I received you.” He keeps the question about the stretcher, but crosses out the unsupported assumption of death.

### 10 · Night Watch Noah · One Stopped Clock Cannot Time Everything

Noah explains that the clock stopped at 03:17 when the power failed. Later witnesses copied the same reading even though they saw you afterward.

You mark those times for checking and retain the order of events, rather than compressing the whole night into a single minute.

### 11 · Broken Horn Abel · Two Directions Across One Bridge

Abel marks the railing and tracks on the map. The same bridge serves the harbor and Fogtown, but travelers face opposite directions.

“First find out why you came back.” You connect the marks to the duty room. The investigation now has a route you can actually follow.

### 12 · Night Watch Noah · An Empty Field Is Not a Grave

A cemetery keeper points at the blank “Found” field and insists you leave your name and stop searching. Noah asks what he actually witnessed.

There is no answer. An unfinished field is no reason to accept the ending he assigns you. You retain the verified record of your return.

### 13 · Empty Hands Finn · A Statement Seeking a Reward

Someone offers a complete signed account if you agree to be called missing. Finn asks which cuff on your coat was patched.

The answer is wrong. Confidence is not a substitute for a witnessed detail, and you will not hand your memory over to a story told for a reward.

### 14 · Broken Horn Abel · Follow the Direction of the Smoke

Abel checks the direction of the firelight against the road in the photograph. The bridge watch points toward the Ash Works route to the harbor.

That establishes an evacuation, not that you started the fire. Abel sends you toward the alarm records rather than treating smoke as an accusation.

### 15 · Empty Hands Finn · Keep the Whole Photograph

Finn retrieves the original print with its border and negative number intact. “Keep more than your face. The people and railing beside you matter too.”

It shows no second version of you. You file the complete print so its location can be checked against the emergency call.

### 16 · Broken Horn Abel · The Bell Tower Comes Next

Abel holds the map beside your lantern and traces the verified stretches between bridge, post office and duty room. A person wearing this coat and carrying this lamp returned along the evacuation road. What happened before that remains unestablished.

Finn brings the final transit slip: the file passed through the Bell Tower. A call index also records a fire alarm mentioning your name. Noah clips his signed sighting on top so new guesses cannot bury established facts. The dented rim can help someone recognize the lantern; it cannot replace those signatures.

“Hear the original words. Then find who spoke them.” Abel points to the tower and holds down a corner of the map against the wind. You lift the lamp and make room for him beside you. The next questions concern the fire call and the last evacuees, not another rumor about your death in the dark streets.

## Bell Tower

### 1 · Night Watch Noah · Hear the Tape with a Traceable Source

The tower has no power. You hang your lantern beside the connection desk so Noah can unfold the duty book and Finn can check the reference copied at the post office. The great clock has stopped. Noah asks for the connection log instead of another account of its chimes.

“The clock stopped. People did not. We need the source of that fire call, not another story attached to 03:17.” By lamplight, Abel checks that the old line’s direction matches the works plan.

On the tape, a voice like yours tells people not to use the main gate. Noah does not identify it at once. He asks you to move the lamp closer to the original notes and hear the words obscured by the alarm. Establish how the speaker wanted people rescued before deciding who spoke.

### 2 · Empty Hands Finn · Keep the Question Specific

The guard talks about who is entitled to enter rather than producing the log. Finn presents the postal reference. “One call. We are not here to cover a shift.”

The guard points to the tape cabinet. You seek the matching box, not every unrelated rumor stored in the tower.

### 3 · Broken Horn Abel · The Original Tape’s Handover

Abel checks the cabinet seals and the receiving and transport marks. The tape box has a traceable number throughout its journey.

You keep access to the original before considering the summaries. If a sentence has been shortened, there will still be something to compare it with.

### 4 · Empty Hands Finn · The Ledger Keeper Wants Another Fee

The ledger keeper demands payment to explain why your name appears. Finn points to the receipt: the post office already paid for the transcription.

The price list disappears. You return to the useful question: how the connection relates to the power failure.

### 5 · Night Watch Noah · A Word Missing from the Summary

Noah finds the full purpose on the transport slip: injured passengers. A shortened copy says only “transport,” which made you imagine bodies.

He keeps both versions. An omitted word cannot decide everyone’s fate; you still need the call arranging the evacuation.

### 6 · Broken Horn Abel · Where the Emergency Supplies Went

Abel shows you the supply entry bearing the fire-alarm batch number. The medicine was for people who had inhaled smoke, not to drug a whole group.

It supports the injury transport record. You retain it as corroboration and return to who requested the service passage.

### 7 · Empty Hands Finn · The Line Reaches the East Furnace

Finn traces the extension to the east-furnace duty room at the Ash Works. There is now a place to visit, rather than an unknown address outside town.

“Do not identify yourself yet.” A similar voice needs corroboration from someone who received the call or saw its speaker.

### 8 · Empty Hands Finn · Finn Restores the Missing Word

Finn puts two transcripts beside the lantern. One says “Use the main gate”; the other begins “Do not.” He will not let you choose the version that feels better. He asks the operator to lay the original notes between them.

The tape strains beneath the noise, but the notes preserve the full instruction: “Do not use the main gate. Open the service passage. Take people to the pier.” You move the lamp so glare on its glass does not obscure the first words. Listening again, you hear the urgent negative beneath the alarm.

“Someone shortened the words to make you accept a different story.” Finn keeps the altered copy for comparison. Familiarity alone cannot identify the voice, but its instruction now describes a route away from the fire. Keep the whole account under the light, and a visible road for anyone following it.

### 9 · Broken Horn Abel · The Passage Really Exists

Abel compares the operator’s notes with the works plan. The service passage leads toward the harbor; the main gate stands beside the furnace area. The caller named a real route.

A map cannot identify a voice. You mark the two doors for checking at the works.

### 10 · Night Watch Noah · One Replay Is Not Another Call

Noah notices that the repeated voice carries the same tape number. “Playback. Not another person calling from the future.”

You group the repeated summaries under one event. The next question concerns the original call, not how often someone replayed it.

### 11 · Broken Horn Abel · A Coat Cannot Establish Guilt

A guard insists that a work coat makes you a shift worker and therefore responsible for the fire. Abel asks for the signed shift list. The guard has none.

You mark the accusation unverified. At the works, you will ask what you actually did, rather than let clothing confess for you.

### 12 · Empty Hands Finn · Leave Room for a Breath

Finn gives the operator water and waits for the coughing to stop. The original call has similar breathless gaps, not an intruding second voice.

You can hear “Take people to the pier.” You record the whole instruction instead of turning a pause into another threat.

### 13 · Broken Horn Abel · The Broken Power Line

Abel checks the burnt power line and the backup battery entry. A recorder running after the outage does not make a prior call a prediction.

That explains its apparent conflict with the stopped clock. You retain the connection sequence instead of retiming every entry by the clock face.

### 14 · Night Watch Noah · Do Not Follow the Altered Account

A collector offers a new copy directing you to another storehouse. Noah notices its extension differs from the original carbon copy.

The source still points to the east-furnace duty room. You keep the altered page behind it so it cannot send another searcher the wrong way.

### 15 · Night Watch Noah · Leave Room Around a Similar Voice

The operator remembers an exhausted caller giving your name. Noah asks him to sign what he heard, not guarantee that the person was you.

That is enough to take to the scene. Voice, name and route can be checked without pretending the remaining uncertainty is settled.

### 16 · Broken Horn Abel · Take the Complete Words to the Works

By your lantern, Abel packs the tape, original notes and works plan in a firm box marked “East-Furnace Duty Room.” Finn bags the altered transcript separately. Noah records where the originals are kept so nobody can later deny they existed.

“Now we know what to ask.” Abel traces the service passage. “Why did the caller know this route? Whom did the people at the door see? Where did the last group go?” The plan stops at what has been checked. He invents no door out of the night.

You test the handle and carry the lantern to the stairs, waiting until everyone has their footing before descending. Ellie's Echo and the site records can test the rescue account. Take the complete words to the works, and ask whom the lamp in the photograph was helping to leave.

## Ash Works

### 1 · Broken Horn Abel · Abel Finds the Right Door

Abel spreads the tower plan outside the works. You light the base of the wall while he locates the service passage and checks its direction. He knows roads, but does not claim to have witnessed the whole night. Even a visible door must agree with the plan and records.

The gate book says you entered through the rescue entrance, took no production tools and joined no shift. Finn notes the arrival before checking the east-furnace duty-room telephone. The soot in your lantern’s glass unsettles you, but does not by itself establish what you did.

“Hearing you ask for a door to open does not mean you started the fire.” Ellie's Echo is willing to explain the passage she held. You raise the lamp so the people behind can see the entrance too. Establish how those entering helped, then whether the last group left.

### 2 · Empty Hands Finn · The Water Slip Reaches the Clinic

Finn finds the water delivery signature. It went to the clinic, with the same batch number as the tower’s injury transport entry.

“Now there is somewhere to ask who drank it.” You follow the slip to the medical record instead of imagining another disposal of personal effects.

### 3 · Empty Hands Finn · The Telephone Is in the Duty Room

Finn matches the door number and extension to the east-furnace duty room. The entry agrees with the tower original; there is no second source bearing the same name.

The request concerns opening the service passage. You mark the confirmed location and seek who carried out that instruction.

### 4 · Ellie's Echo · An Injury Record Is Not a Death Record

Ellie's Echo gives you a medical entry for smoke inhalation and a burned arm, followed by a discharge signature. She asks you to drink some warm water first.

“This records someone who was treated.” You were alive then. Next, establish where you went after leaving the clinic.

### 5 · Broken Horn Abel · Not a Replacement Shift Worker

Your name is absent from the shift issue sheet but present at the rescue entrance. Abel connects the two: you did not come to replace a missing worker.

“We still need what you did.” He leaves the next line open so that limited finding does not substitute for the evacuation itself.

### 6 · Ellie's Echo · Count the People Leaving

Ellie's Echo unfolds the evacuation sheet: people walking, needing assistance or needing stretchers. Each category concerns rescue, not remains.

She asks you to check the handover out of the works. A count inside the door still needs someone to establish who led them outside.

### 7 · Broken Horn Abel · The Negative Keeps the Full Scene

Abel brings the full print from the darkroom. You stand outside the service exit supporting an injured person, the hand lamp at your wrist and blue stitching visible at your cuff.

The surrounding negative numbers are continuous. He keeps the other people in frame so the closing of that door can be checked next.

### 8 · Empty Hands Finn · Finn Does Not Hand Over the Original

Someone blocks you, claiming the sealed service door proves nobody could have escaped and demanding the photograph for destruction. Finn puts the original inside his coat and offers a numbered copy. You set the lantern by the door so both sides can read it.

He asks Ellie's Echo when the door closed. She shows you where the supports stood and checks the roll call: the passage opened for evacuation and was sealed against the fire after the last group passed. The seal, opening and closure are different facts. One moment cannot stand for the whole night.

“A copy is enough if he only wants to check.” You lift the lamp and light the ground as Ellie crosses the threshold, then follow the sequence through to the handover. One vague claim cannot erase the living people in the photograph.

### 9 · Broken Horn Abel · This Passage Leads to the Harbor

Abel follows the service passage to the coal walkway ending at Sunken Harbor. He crosses out the fire-blocked main gate and retains the usable route.

You know where to ask next. First establish the door’s sequence, then take the handover to the people who received the group at the pier.

### 10 · Broken Horn Abel · Do Not Read the Seal Backward

The person who sealed the door claims the surviving seal means nobody passed and urges you back to town. Abel points to the inspection order: sealing followed the last group’s departure.

“You left out the earlier part.” You keep both records and continue toward who received those people.

### 11 · Empty Hands Finn · The Carbon Copy Survived

Finn finds a smoke-darkened carbon copy whose number still matches the original. He handles its edge through a dry cloth so it will not tear in your hurry.

It corroborates the departure direction. You still need the receiving signature; recovering one paper does not answer the whole journey.

### 12 · Empty Hands Finn · Someone Wants the Handover Burned

Someone pushes handover sheets toward a brazier, claiming their destruction will end questions about the omitted names. Finn retrieves an unlit copy.

“You want your own trouble closed.” Its number leads from the last group to the pier, which is precisely why it must not be discarded.

### 13 · Empty Hands Finn · Your Blue-Stitched Coat

Finn asks you to turn the cuff over. Ellie's Echo recognizes the needle and thread she supplied; you recognize the backward knot you tied while patching it in a hurry.

The borrowed-uniform tag beside it belongs to another coat. You separate it instead of accepting a shift assigned by someone else’s clothes.

### 14 · Empty Hands Finn · A Rescue Request Is Not a Furnace Order

Someone uses the name on the call to blame you for the fire. Finn reads the instruction to its end: open the evacuation passage, not the furnace door.

The omitted words describe the route you have verified. You keep the complete transcript and continue checking the handover out of the works.

### 15 · Broken Horn Abel · The Furnace Area Ends at This Door

Abel matches the ash boundary to the fire door on the evacuation plan. Danger remained inside; the route outside toward the harbor was not blocked by that same fire.

The photograph and roll call now share a location. You take the departure record to Ellie's Echo before seeking its receiving copy at the pier.

### 16 · Ellie's Echo · Ellie's Echo Takes You to the Gate

Ellie's Echo dries a place for the departure slip and asks you to put the lantern beside the photograph. The same dented rim and sooty corner appear there. She recognizes how you raised it ahead of the group and turned to help an injured traveler, but still has you read the roll call and handover.

“While I hold the lantern, the people behind me still have a way.” She looks at you. “That was what you said. I held the door; you led the last group through the service passage. We sealed the furnace area afterward.” Abel’s route, Finn’s originals and her own sighting now describe the same stretch.

The next entry names the harbor’s final evacuation boat, but Ellie does not sign for the pier. She clears ash from the shade’s air holes and returns your lantern. “Beyond the gate, I could not see. Light the road ahead. Ask whom the boat received, then whether they arrived.” You pack the originals, knowing which stretch remains.

## Sunken Harbor

### 1 · Broken Horn Abel · Find the Receiving Desk

Abel shelters the factory handover inside his coat and leads you along the coal walkway to the waiting shelter. You hold the lantern between you, lighting the wet boards so those behind do not lose their footing while staring at the water. He stops at a desk bearing the factory transfer sign.

“This is where the factory sent them. Find who received them before deciding somebody fell into the water.” He lifts the tarpaulin with his broken horn so you can turn the pages in the dry. You move the lamp beside the receiving field. Its batch number matches the factory sheet, but your name is absent from the seat list.

Abel separates the sheets instead of filling the gap for you: he will check the approach, you will identify the boat, and Finn will find the copyist. You take lantern and file into the shelter, remembering Ellie’s account. Your help at the works is established; one omitted line cannot decide the rest.

### 2 · Broken Horn Abel · Count People Before Tickets

Abel obtains an unused ticket form and sets it beside the seat list. Each ticket covers one numbered seat; there is no field for crew or temporary helpers.

“One missing ticket is not yet one missing person.” He marks the gap and sends you to the handling records, rather than the waiting crowd's guesses about fare dodgers.

### 3 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · The Stretcher Used That Space

Eli is sorting rescue ropes when you mention the missing name. He points to an alteration on the seating plan: chairs were moved so an injured traveler could lie flat.

He confirms only what he saw. You steadied the stretcher and did not leave the boat because of it. The crew's working copy must establish the crossing; a seat cannot answer for you.

### 4 · Broken Horn Abel · A Knot Used for Lifting

A worn lifebuoy hangs in the shelter. Someone claims it proves a death that night. Abel turns the rim over and finds a stretcher-handling number matching the factory handover.

“Being beside water does not make every object evidence of drowning.” He puts it back. You need the people who lifted the stretcher aboard, not another victim invented for a rumor.

### 5 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · The Last Boat's Working Copy

Eli takes you to the captain and gives the transfer number before asking him to recognize a face. The last boat's working copy has a separate helper entry on its reverse.

Water has blurred the writing, but somebody outside the seat list was tending the injured. Eli asks you to keep the whole page and find why the copied list omitted it.

### 6 · Empty Hands Finn · The Hidden Reverse

Nico pins down the seat list, insists an absent name means you never boarded, and tries to remove the working copy while you look away. Finn catches its corner.

“Did you omit the helpers to dodge blame, or to stop us checking?” Instead of arguing, he asks the captain for the original. You record the alteration rather than accepting a selectively copied conclusion.

### 7 · Broken Horn Abel · Only the Seat Was Given Up

Abel has the handlers demonstrate the narrow aisle using the stretcher's fixing points. You can see why giving up your numbered place did not leave room for another chair.

Once the stretcher was secured, you remained beside the crew. Abel writes that down for the handlers to verify. Having no seat and making no crossing are now clearly different claims.

### 8 · Empty Hands Finn · Finn Will Not Invent a Ticket

Finn returns from the copying room with a curled carbon sheet. He asks you to steady the lantern before reading the impression beneath the helper entry: your name, followed by “aboard with the last passenger group.”

“I can recover an omitted line. I cannot invent a seat you never had.” A crew member checks the entry and remembers you on that same boat, hanging the lantern beside the stretcher so its straps could be secured again. The action agrees with the working copy; it does not establish the later cart transfer.

Finn gives the original to the captain for verification and keeps the wrong list too. You lift the lantern that now lights the boat record and check the landing together: establish that you traveled, and exactly where the helpers’ entry was left out.

### 9 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · Light Cannot Sign for a Passenger

Eli hands you the lamp's wiring log. The early alert came through a recorded-message line marked White Room; the light on the water was the lamp's reflection.

“Ask the sending end when we reach it. First establish where the people went.” He files the line reference beneath the boat record. A received signal cannot replace an arrival receipt.

### 10 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · Do Not Supply a Liar's Witness

A patrolman urges you to sign “left the boat voluntarily,” claiming it will be simpler. Eli moves the pen aside and asks whom he actually saw entering the water.

The man admits it was hearsay. You do not sign. With the crew's original record in hand, an excuse cannot erase you from the crossing.

### 11 · Empty Hands Finn · Changing Sides, Not People

Finn finds the same helper mark in two places. A crewman explains that you moved from the stern to the other side to catch a landing rope; both entries record work.

He checks the name and order instead of inventing a second you. The remaining gap begins after landing. Finn asks for the window that handled the transfer carts.

### 12 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · Confirm Only What Was Seen

Eli moves “arrived safely at the station” back into the unverified column. “I saw you board, and I saw the boat land. I did not enter the station afterward.”

That narrows the question rather than undoing the progress. He signs for the crossing and leaves the receiving entry to the people who were actually there.

### 13 · Broken Horn Abel · The Transfer Number Leads to Snow Station

Abel finds a cart-transfer counterfoil matching the last boat's batch. Its destination is Snow Station reception. You connect the landing steps to the station on your map.

“Landing and arriving at the station are still one stretch apart.” He copies the number and keeps the factory sheet with it, so the receiving staff can check the same group.

### 14 · Empty Hands Finn · A Receipt Cannot Be Bought

A man beside the transfer desk offers to complete the receiving signature for a fee. Finn pushes the blank form back, his smile gone.

“We need whoever received these travelers, not a hand that can imitate a signature.” You leave with the verified transfer number, buying neither false reassurance nor forged evidence.

### 15 · Broken Horn Abel · Three Records, One Journey

At the warehouse door, Abel aligns the factory handover, crew copy and cart counterfoil. The batch numbers connect. No extra boat or swim across the harbor is needed.

Your finger follows the map to Snow Station reception and its missing receiving signature. Abel repacks the papers. This actual gap, rather than a rumor, gives you the next destination.

### 16 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · Take the Boat Record to Snow Station

Eli puts his own navigation lamp by the window to clear space for the boat record’s verification stamp. Then he tightens the loose handle clasp on your portable lantern. The two lights have different jobs; names and sailings still require the book. A visible light cannot prove someone reached shore.

You can now describe the crossing: you yielded a numbered seat, stayed aboard the last boat, lit the stretcher straps and helped transfer the injured after landing. “I will give the captain an accurate report.” Eli puts a dry cloth in your document bag. “At Snow Station, find the receiving window, not just the ticket book.”

He signs only what he verified, without declaring everyone safe in advance. You take lantern, record copy and transfer number. Next establish who received the group and where you went afterward. Carry the light ahead; let the records follow the journey one stretch at a time.

## Snow Station

### 1 · Ellie's Echo · Warm Your Hands First

Ellie's Echo is heating water at Snow Station’s temporary clinic. You arrive with the lantern in one hand and damp papers in the other. She finds a secure hook for the light, then puts a cup in your hands, waiting until you can untie the bundle before asking about the harbor.

You explain that you gave up your seat but stayed aboard to help. She recognizes the blue cuff. “I gave you the needle and thread. You tied the knot backward yourself. A missing stretch of memory does not erase the person who stopped to help.” The lantern burns between you. Your promise to those behind no longer depends on your own recollection alone.

Ellie sends the transfer number to Alma at reception. Once your hands are warm, you take down the lamp and read the names page by page. Establish whether the group entered the station and whether you came with them, without signing for a road not yet checked.

### 2 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · From the Landing to Reception

Eli gives the station the cart counterfoil, checking the batch before asking where it went. The route matches Abel's map: from the landing, the stretcher went into the waiting room.

He circles reception. The crossing has already been established; there is no need to start again with tickets. Find who brought the injured indoors and signed for them.

### 3 · Ellie's Echo · A Transfer Cart, Not a Train

Ellie's Echo reads “did not get on the cart” twice and checks with a stretcher handler. It refers to the empty cart returning to the landing; you did not ride it back.

It says nothing about the evacuation boat. You clarify the cart's purpose beside the original note, rather than letting an ambiguous word overturn the verified crossing.

### 4 · Ellie's Echo · Evacuees Do Not Need Another Ticket

A window guard refuses to open the retained ledger unless you buy a ticket. Ellie's Echo points to the evacuation reception instructions: this group was registered for assistance, not a train fare.

She tells you to keep your money and search by transfer batch. The guard can redirect an inconvenience to the ticket office, but cannot produce a train boarding record.

### 5 · Frozen Sentry Alma · Record Where the Broadcast Came From

Alma takes a routing card from the radio and marks the old broadcast's origin: the White Room. The voice says, “Do not report yourself missing again. Bring the arrival receipt.”

She seals the original recording instead of treating a familiar voice as proof. “Start with the part we can check. Find the receipt, then ask who recorded it.” The warning now gives you a definite task.

### 6 · Ellie's Echo · The Blankets Reached the Clinic

Ellie's Echo matches the blanket issue tag to the harbor batch. A handler remembers you wrapping an injured traveler at the entrance, then clearing the empty stretcher from the way.

That connects with the person she saw at the factory gate, but says nothing about the White Room yet. She records this stretch and takes you to the clinic's receiving page.

### 7 · Frozen Sentry Alma · Two Helpers, One Stretcher

Alma finds the same stretcher on two sheets and checks who handed it over and who received it, rather than counting two missing travelers. Your name is on the accompanying-guide side.

She keeps both signatures with their separate functions. Two people handling one stretcher do not create an extra passenger. The same batch can now be followed into the station.

### 8 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · Eli Leaves the Uncertain Part Open

Eli dries the last page of the boat record beside the radiator. You set the lantern on the table corner and compare the landing with the cart transfer. He asks you to move the shade’s shadow so every name can be read in full.

When you begin writing “everyone reached the White Room,” he stops the pen. “I can confirm the landing and stretcher handover. I did not follow them inside. Reassuring you is no reason to claim another stretch.” He lays Alma’s station records separately, letting each witness establish only their own part.

The crossing remains solid. The remaining gap is the path from waiting room to White Room. Eli returns the book for you to check with the people who stayed inside. You lift the lamp, making room for established facts and leaving unverified fields open. A gap in your evidence does not erase the part already established.

### 9 · Frozen Sentry Alma · Separate Arrival from Onward Transfer

Alma separates the arrival and onward-transfer pages, finding you with the evacuees on the first before opening the second. You can now confirm that you reached Snow Station alive.

The later note points toward the White Room without signing for its reception staff. She has you keep the two claims distinct: a confirmed arrival and a recorded direction are different kinds of evidence.

### 10 · Frozen Sentry Alma · Do Not Trade Your Name for a False Receipt

Soren offers a completed receipt if you cross out your name, claiming nobody will question you afterward. Alma asks who received the group and compares the stamp with the ledger.

It is a blank from another batch, not proof of your journey. She returns it. Removing your name would remove evidence; it would not find you an exit.

### 11 · Ellie's Echo · The Same Coat Was Collected

Ellie's Echo finds your coat on the collection sheet. It was dried in the waiting room and collected again; the note records the blue cuff. There is no second identical coat awaiting an owner.

She smiles at your unchanged backward knot, then points to the departure order. The wearer went toward the White Room. Record the direction, then find somebody there who saw you arrive.

### 12 · Lighthouse Warden Eli · Do Not Wait on the Platform for a Footpath

A station guard tells you to wait on the platform, hoping to remove the troublesome questions from reception. Eli opens the map: the White Room lies beyond the footpath on the other side of the station.

Two people who saw you leave confirm that no train was involved. Eli folds the map and sends you along the verified route instead of substituting endless waiting for the next inquiry.

### 13 · Ellie's Echo · Your Answer Is in the Medical Note

Ellie's Echo opens the temporary clinic's receiving page. Smoke had left you hoarse, but you answered your name and asked whether the injured travelers were warm enough.

Your hand relaxes when you see the signature for hot water. This page cannot answer everything, but it settles the fear that you never reached the station alive.

### 14 · Frozen Sentry Alma · Hear the Warning to Its End
